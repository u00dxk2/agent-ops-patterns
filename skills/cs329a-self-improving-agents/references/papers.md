# Papers - the primary sources

Every URL below was opened and matched to its title on 2026-08-28. CI re-checks that each one still resolves (`test/skill-references.test.mjs`, the `links` job); it cannot check that the page is still the paper, so re-open a row before you quote it outward.

**Part** = the video in the nine-part series where the lecturer discussed the paper (read from the lecture transcripts). The course's own 20-lecture syllabus at https://cs329a.stanford.edu/ numbers things differently and assigns more readings than the videos cover; this manifest is the videos' set.

**Idea** = the numbered idea in `ideas.md` the paper backs; `-` = discussed in the videos and cited from `SKILL.md`, but not the basis of a numbered idea.

| Part | Paper | Authors / year | URL | Central claim | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling | Brown et al., 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787 | Sampling the same model many times and picking a correct answer improves problem-solving far more than a single attempt; inference compute is its own scaling axis, and coverage follows a power law when the task set has a long tail of hard problems. | 1 |
| 2 | Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters | Snell et al., 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314 | A fixed inference budget spent according to prompt difficulty (sequential revision for easy, parallel search for hard) beats spending the same compute on a larger model - except on the hardest tail. | 10 |
| 2 | Archon: An Architecture Search Framework for Inference-Time Techniques | Saad-Falcon et al., 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15254 | Combining inference-time techniques (sampling, critique, ranking, fusion) is a search problem; automated search over that space beats hand-assembly, and fusing the top candidates beats selecting the best one. | 9 |
| 2 | KernelBench: Can LLMs Write Efficient GPU Kernels? | Ouyang et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10517 | A benchmark where correctness is verified by output equivalence against the PyTorch reference - the "translation is verifiable" case the lecture uses. | 1 |
| 3 | Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems | Cobbe et al., 2021 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168 | Introduces GSM8K; a trained verifier ranking many samples beats fine-tuning the generator harder, and verifier benefit saturates then reverses as sample count grows past its resolution. | 1, 10 |
| 3 | Let's Verify Step by Step | Lightman et al., 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050 | Process supervision (a reward per reasoning step) trains more reliable models than outcome supervision and catches right-answer-wrong-process; releases PRM800K. | 5, 9 |
| 3 | Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations | Wang et al., 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08935 | Step-level reward labels can be generated automatically by rolling out from each step, removing the human-annotation bottleneck for process rewards. | 5 |
| 3 | Shrinking the Generation-Verification Gap with Weak Verifiers (Weaver) | Saad-Falcon et al., 2025 (NeurIPS 2025) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18203 | Many weak, imperfect verifiers combine into one strong verifier via weak supervision on very few labels - after filtering out the bad ones; naive rubric-prompted multi-judge voting underperforms majority vote. | 2 |
| 4 | ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models | Yao et al., 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629 | Interleaving reasoning traces with tool actions in one loop beats either alone; grounding through actions cuts hallucination. | 6 |
| 4 | RLEF: Grounding Code LLMs in Execution Feedback with Reinforcement Learning | Gehring et al., 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02089 | Training a code model with RL on actual execution feedback teaches it to use the feedback; public tests to iterate against, private tests to score on. | 5 |
| 4 | Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback | Bai et al., 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073 | A model can be trained toward a written set of principles by critiquing and revising its own outputs against them; the lecture notes self-critique is weaker than critique by another model. | 8 |
| 4 | CodeMonkeys: Scaling Test-Time Compute for Software Engineering | Ehrlich et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723 | Scaling both candidate generation and candidate selection at test time - with model-generated tests as the selector - moves real issue resolution. | 1, 5 |
| 5 | Language Agent Tree Search Unifies Reasoning Acting and Planning in Language Models (LATS) | Zhou et al., 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406 | Monte-Carlo tree search over an agent's reasoning and actions, with environment feedback and self-reflection as the value signal; irreversible actions are out of scope. | - (cited in SKILL.md § What the course argues against) |
| 5 | SPRINT: Enabling Interleaved Planning and Parallelized Execution in Reasoning Models | Biju et al., 2025 (NeurIPS 2025) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05745 | Reasoning models can be post-trained to find independent steps in their own chain of thought and run them in parallel, cutting sequential tokens without losing accuracy. | - |
| 5 | Synthetic Data Generation & Multi-Step RL for Reasoning & Tool Use (SWiRL) | Goldie et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04736 | Multi-step RL over synthetic trajectories with a process reward on each action (judged before the tool answers) generalizes across tasks and tools; process-filtered data beat outcome-filtered. | 5 |
| 6 | STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning | Zelikman et al., 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14465 | A model bootstraps its own reasoning by keeping rationales that led to correct answers, and rationalizing failures given the answer as a hint. | 4 |
| 6 | DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models | Shao et al., 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300 | A 7B open model reaches near-frontier math via math-heavy pretraining plus GRPO, a critic-free PPO variant; the lecture's read is that it improved consistency (majority@K), not coverage (pass@K). | 4 |
| 6 | DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale | Yu et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476 | Open-sources the techniques behind frontier RL reasoning results, including dynamic sampling - dropping prompts where every sample passes or every sample fails, because neither carries a gradient. | 4 |
| 7 | Competition-Level Code Generation with AlphaCode | Li et al., 2022 (Science) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07814 | Massive sampling plus filtering by example tests and clustering by behaviour reaches median-competitor level; the selection stage, not generation, is the bottleneck. | 1, 9 |
| 7 | AlphaCode 2 Technical Report | AlphaCode Team, Google DeepMind, 2023 | https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/AlphaCode2/AlphaCode2_Tech_Report.pdf | A stronger base model plus a learned scoring model solves 1.7× more problems than AlphaCode at a fraction of the samples - 85th percentile of human competitors. | 1, 10 |
| 7 | Search-o1: Agentic Search-Enhanced Large Reasoning Models | Li et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05366 | A reasoning model that retrieves mid-chain, with a separate module that extracts the relevant span from retrieved documents, fixes the knowledge gaps that derail its reasoning; appending whole documents made it worse. | 7 |
| 7 | Search-R1: Training LLMs to Reason and Leverage Search Engines with Reinforcement Learning | Jin et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516 | Prompting a model to search is suboptimal; RL over the search-interaction policy itself does better. | 7 |
| 8 | Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks (v1 title: "…Long Tasks"; retitled in v3) | Kwa et al., 2025 (METR) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499 | Defines the 50%-task-completion time horizon - the human task length a model completes half the time - and finds it doubling roughly every seven months; the 80% horizon is much shorter. | 6 |
| 8 | GDPval: Evaluating AI Model Performance on Real-World Economically Valuable Tasks | Patwardhan et al., 2025 (OpenAI) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04374 | Benchmarks models on real deliverables from 44 occupations; frontier models approach expert quality roughly linearly, with instruction-following the top failure mode. | 6 |
| 8 | DeepScholar-Bench: A Live Benchmark and Automated Evaluation for Generative Research Synthesis | Patel et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20033 | A continuously refreshed benchmark for systems that search the live web and write cited research syntheses; no evaluated system exceeded 19% on the combined bar. | 7 |
| 9 | Multiagent Finetuning: Self Improvement with Diverse Reasoning Chains | Subramaniam et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05707 | Self-improvement plateaus for a single model as its outputs lose diversity; fine-tuning a society of specialized copies preserves diversity and keeps improvement going. | 8 |
| 9 | DeepSeekMath-V2: Towards Self-Verifiable Mathematical Reasoning | Shao et al., 2025 (DeepSeek) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22570 | Training a proof verifier alongside the generator - with a meta-verifier checking whether the verifier's named issues exist - and rewarding self-verified proofs rather than final answers. | 3 |
| 9 | Absolute Zero: Reinforced Self-play Reasoning with Zero Data | Zhao et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335 | A model proposes its own tasks and solves them with a code executor as the reward, scoring proposals at the edge of its ability - zero for trivial or impossible. | 4 |
| 9 | Intelligence per Watt: Measuring Intelligence Efficiency of Local AI | Saad-Falcon et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07885 | Introduces intelligence-per-watt for local inference; small local models now handle a large share of real queries at far better energy efficiency than cloud frontier models. | 10 |

- Verified means: the URL was loaded and the page's title matched the row. Venue notes (NeurIPS 2025 for Weaver and SPRINT) are as shown on the arXiv page; SWiRL's venue is not stated on its page and is not claimed here.

- AlphaCode 2 is not on arXiv; the DeepMind-hosted PDF is the official page.
